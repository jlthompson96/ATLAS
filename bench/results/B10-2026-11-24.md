## Shape of the day

| | MB |
|---|---:|
| Busiest 10 minutes: the first interval, 15:03Z, 8 minutes after the deploy | 4.7 |
| Total without that interval | 31.4 |
| Hours 15:00–16:59Z on day 1 (after the deploy) | 13.0 |
| A typical hour later on | 0.6–0.7 |
| Median 10-minute interval | 0.0 |
| 90th percentile 10-minute interval | 0.69 |

- **Steady state is about 0.6 MB/hour, or about 15 MB/day.** The rest is bursts: the two hours after the deploy, about 3.9 MB around 19:00–19:30Z on day 1, and about 2 MB around 11:00Z on day 2. The bursts after 17:00Z are not explained. Possible causes are apt or man-db timers, logrotate, or SSH sessions during the evening's work.
- The first interval is most likely writeback left over from the deploy, which the run didn't count as a deploy. Deploys are measured separately (pi-verification.md, "A deploy's write volume"). Even counting it, the day is within budget.
- **Journal:** `du` of `/var/log/journal` stayed at 25,564 KiB from start to end. journald preallocates its files in 8 MiB steps, so `du` doesn't show writes inside an allocated file. It shows only that the journal didn't need a new chunk this day. Its share of the 36 MB isn't measured here.
- **Audit:** not measured separately. The audit directory is root-only, and no `sudo du` was taken at the start. The audit service was idle apart from its own checkpoints, so this run doesn't test the audit estimate of 1–20 MB/day under load.

## Limits

- These are host writes, not the card's internal writes, so the budget is a design target, not an endurance figure.
- The result holds only for this configuration. Rerun B10 when services that write are added: the backup (runs from the USB drive, but its status file is on the card), `atlas-core`/`atlas-ui`, the memory DB and the queue (Phases 1–4), and the kiosk (Chromium's cache is in a RuntimeDirectory, so it should not write to the card).
- One day, one card. The alert in §3.5 (over budget two days running) is not built yet.
